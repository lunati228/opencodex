Analyze the following systems problem carefully and quantitatively.

A mixture-of-experts model has 60 routed layers. Each layer owns 512 experts and
selects 10 experts per token. One complete cached expert slot includes its gate,
up, and down weights. A machine can reserve a fixed number of slots per layer in
VRAM. Cache hits run on a GPU while misses run on the CPU, and the two paths can
overlap, but the token cannot finish until both paths and their synchronization
complete. Expert popularity can change between prompts and over time.

Develop a rigorous method for deciding whether a persistent VRAM expert cache is
worth building. Compare LRU, aged LFU, segmented LRU, and an offline frequency
oracle. Derive the measurements needed to separate cold, warming, and steady
behavior. Explain how to calculate hit rate, churn, per-layer percentiles, traffic
coverage, and an honest latency bound without confusing raw model residency,
Windows page-cache hits, and VRAM compute hits. Give counterexamples where a high
aggregate hit rate still fails to improve tokens per second. End with a
pre-registered decision table and a verification plan that protects model
fidelity. Show equations, assumptions, edge cases, and uncertainty explicitly.
